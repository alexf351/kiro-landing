AI learning statistics 2026: how beginners actually learn AI

By Alex Furukawa, founder of Iro. Last updated November 17, 2026.

Most AI literacy statistics come from surveys that ask people what they think they know. This page shows what beginners actually do when they learn AI, from lesson data in Iro, an app that teaches AI skills in short daily lessons on iPhone and the web. It covers 339 learners who finished at least one lesson between April 30 and November 17, 2026.

Key findings

  • Beginners who asked an AI tutor a question on their first day were 1.7x as likely to come back (62% vs 36%).
  • Learners who finished two lessons on day one came back 58% of the time, against 42% after one lesson.
  • 37% of people who finished a first AI lesson never finished a second.
  • In timed quizzes, answers given in under 8 seconds were right 71% of the time, against 59% for slower answers.
  • When people could build a lesson on anything, 57% chose a topic other than AI.

1. Asking the AI tutor on day one: 62% came back, vs 36%

1.7x

Beginners who asked Iro's AI tutor a question on their first day came back to learn on a later day 62% of the time. Learners who never asked the tutor anything came back 36% of the time. That makes first-day askers 1.7x as likely to return. The question the most learners asked was "Why was mine wrong?", ahead of "Give an example" and "Quiz me".

Sample: 50 learners who asked on day one and 234 who never asked, all with a first lesson before November 10, 2026. This is a correlation: curious learners may both ask more and come back more.

2. Two lessons on day one: 58% came back, vs 42% after one

Learners who finished two or more lessons on their first day came back 58% of the time. Learners who stopped after one came back 42% of the time.

3. 37% never finish a second AI lesson

The biggest drop-off comes right after the first lesson. 37% of people who finished their first AI lesson never finished a second one. 34% reached five lessons, 18% reached ten and 6% reached 25. Learners who did return usually came back the very next day (median gap: one day).

4. Quick answers were right 71% of the time, vs 59% for slower ones

In Iro's 15-second head-to-head quiz duels, answers given in under 8 seconds were right 71% of the time. Answers that took 8 seconds or more were right 59% of the time. Hesitating didn't help.

5. Given free rein, 57% chose a topic other than AI

When learners could build a custom lesson on any topic, 57% chose something other than AI, such as cooking, personal finance, fitness or job interviews. 73% of those lessons were set to beginner level. People use AI to learn everything else.

Also in the data

Weekends were quieter: Saturdays and Sundays averaged about 19% fewer active learners than weekdays.

What this means if you're learning AI

  • Do two short lessons on your first day, not one.
  • Ask questions as you go, especially "why was mine wrong?"
  • Plan for weekdays, when the habit seems easiest to keep.
  • Go with your first read on a question, then check the explanation.

Method

  • Source: Iro's own lesson, tutor, duel and custom-lesson records, aggregated. No personal data appears on this page.
  • Learners: accounts that finished at least one lesson between April 30 and November 17, 2026 (339 learners). Replays are excluded.
  • Came back: finished a lesson on a later calendar day than the first one. Return comparisons only include learners whose first lesson was at least a week before the data was pulled.
  • Asked on day one: asked the AI tutor a question on or before the day of the learner's first lesson.
  • Excluded: the founder's own and test accounts, and the app store review account.
  • Duels: 15-second multiple-choice rounds. Answers that ran out the clock are excluded from the speed comparison.

Limitations

These are people who chose to download a learning app, on iPhone and the web, so they are not a random sample of the public. The data has no demographics. Return rates are correlations, not proof of cause. Findings 4 and 5 rest on fewer than 30 people each and are labeled as early patterns. We will refresh this page every quarter and note what changed.

FAQ

How many people quit learning AI after one lesson?

In Iro's data, 37% of people who finished a first AI lesson never finished a second (126 of 339 learners, April 30 to November 17, 2026).

What helps beginners stick with learning AI?

Two first-day habits stood out. Learners who asked the AI tutor a question on day one were 1.7x as likely to come back (62% vs 36%), and learners who finished two lessons on day one came back 58% of the time against 42% for one.

How long does it take to build an AI learning habit?

Most learners who came back did so the very next day (median gap: one day), so the first 48 hours matter most. See how long it takes to learn AI.

Do people learn AI more on weekends?

No. In Iro's data, weekend days averaged about 19% fewer active learners than weekdays.

Where does this data come from?

From anonymized, aggregated lesson records of 339 Iro learners between April 30 and November 17, 2026. The founder's own and test accounts and the app store review account are excluded.

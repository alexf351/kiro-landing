The best Headway alternatives in 2026

Headway and Blinkist do the same job: a book, compressed, in about fifteen minutes. If you are looking for an alternative, the useful question is whether you want a better version of that job or a different job entirely.

By Alex FurukawaPublished 2026-08-18~6 min readAI Fluency

Key takeaways

  • If you want book summaries and just want a different app, Blinkist is the direct equivalent and the choice comes down to interface and library.
  • If summaries stopped translating into anything you actually do, the format is the issue and switching summary apps will not fix it.
  • Iro AI is the alternative if the skill you want is using AI well: five-minute sessions built around exercises rather than chapters.
  • Khan Academy is entirely free and is the honest answer if the real constraint is budget.

The best Headway alternatives

Blinkist if you want the same thing done differently. Something else entirely if summaries have stopped working for you. Those two apps are close substitutes, and choosing between them is a question of interface, library and price rather than approach.

The more useful fork is the second one. If you have a shelf of finished summaries and nothing about your week has changed, the answer is not a third summary app. It is a product where the session is a thing you do rather than a thing you read.

Why people look for a Headway alternative

Three reasons, in roughly the order they come up.

  • Billing surprise. Common across this whole category. Whatever you switch to, find the renewal price and the renewal date before you subscribe.
  • Narration. Some reviewers find the audio delivery flat. That is a matter of taste rather than a defect, but it is a real reason people move, and it is worth sampling the audio before committing.
  • Nothing stuck. The important one. Summaries are excellent at coverage and poor at transfer, because knowing an argument and having a habit are different things.

Where a summary app still wins

  • Editorially produced range. Thousands of professionally summarised titles against a curated AI library. Iro is not limited to AI — Custom Paths (a Pro feature) generate a path on any topic you name, from a typed subject, a link, a screenshot or a PDF — but a generated path is not the same as a book condensed by an editor.

Iro closes part of this: lesson podcasts play any lesson as a two-to-three-minute episode with two hosts, so you can take the lesson on a commute and do the practice later. They are Pro, live on iOS and the web, and Headway's catalogue of narrated summaries is still bigger. If listening is the only slot you will ever have, that is a genuine argument for staying.

Iro also runs as a free web app at app.tryiro.com with no signup, which is the quickest way to find out whether the format fits.

How to choose

Ask what you want to be true in a month.

If it is "I will have read about twenty more things," a summary app delivers that reliably and you should pick on library and interface. If it is "I will do one specific thing better," you need a product built around repetition and feedback, and you should pick the one that specialises in that thing.

Then check the free tier properly. Not whether it exists, but whether it lets you do the real activity. A free tier that only shows you a tour tells you nothing about whether you will still be using the app in March.

FAQ

What is the best Headway alternative?

Blinkist is the closest like-for-like swap, since both compress books into short summaries. If the reason you are leaving is that summaries have not changed anything you do, the better answer is a practice-based app in whatever skill you care about: Iro AI for using AI tools well, Brilliant or Khan Academy for maths and science foundations.

Headway vs Blinkist, which is better?

They do the same job, so the honest answer is that it comes down to library, interface and current pricing rather than approach. Sample both. If neither is producing a change in what you actually do, the choice between them is not the decision that matters.

Is there a free Headway alternative?

Khan Academy is entirely free. Iro AI is free to start on iOS with every path open, paced by a Battery that gives you a lesson a day and recharges overnight, and it runs as a free web app at app.tryiro.com with no signup. Compare what each free tier lets you actually do, since the word covers very different things in this category.

Why do book summaries not seem to stick?

Because a summary transfers an argument, and arguments do not become habits by themselves. The format is optimised for coverage: it gets you the shape of a book in fifteen minutes. Building a skill needs repetition with feedback, which is a different activity and a different kind of product. Finishing lots of summaries and changing nothing is the format working exactly as designed.

Is Iro AI like Headway?

Only in session length. Both ask about five minutes a day. Headway gives you a compressed book; Iro gives you an exercise with instant feedback, across 29 curated paths about using AI tools. If your goal is broad reading through professionally condensed books, Headway is the better fit. Iro is not confined to AI — Custom Paths (a Pro feature) generate a path on any topic you name, from a typed subject, a link, a screenshot or a PDF — but it is a practice product, not a reading library.

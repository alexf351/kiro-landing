The best Kinnu alternatives

Answer the fork first. Kinnu the general-knowledge app and Kinnu's exam-prep platform for the UK SQE1 and CFA Level I are separate products, and swapping one for the other's alternative is the most common mistake on this query.

If you were reading pathways on history, science and philosophy for general interest, you are looking for another curated microlearning app, and the right one depends on the subject: Iro AI if what you actually want is to use AI tools well, Brilliant for maths, logic and computer science, Khan Academy for academic foundations at no cost.

If you were studying for a qualification, none of those replace what you had. A syllabus-aligned question bank with thousands of practice questions is a specialist product, and the alternative to one is another one, not a general knowledge app.

Why people look for a Kinnu alternative

Three reasons come up, and they point in different directions.

  • They finished the pathways they cared about. Curated libraries are finite by design, which is a strength while you are in one and a problem when you run out. The fix is a different subject, not a different app.
  • They want a skill rather than knowledge. General knowledge is genuinely enjoyable and makes you better company. It does not change what you can do on a Tuesday morning. If that gap is what is bothering you, you want a product where the session is an exercise rather than a reading.
  • They are on Android and want the general-knowledge app there. Kinnu's exam-prep platform is on Google Play; availability of the general-knowledge app outside iOS is worth checking on the store yourself, because store titles in this family shift between regions.

Only the second is a criticism, and even then it is a criticism of the category rather than the app. Curated general knowledge does exactly what it says.

If you were using the exam-prep platform

Different search entirely. Kinnu's SQE1 product ships a bank of 3,000+ multiple-choice questions, 350+ study notes, 2,000+ flashcards and a context-aware AI tutor called LARA, and that shape, a large syllabus-mapped question bank with analytics, is what you should be comparing against. Ask any candidate replacement three things: how many questions, mapped to which syllabus version, and does it show you a weakness breakdown rather than a score. Nothing on the table above answers those questions, including us.

How to choose

Three questions, in order.

  • Knowledge or skill? If you want to understand more things, stay in the general-knowledge category and pick on library. If you want to do one thing better, leave it.
  • Curated or generated? A curated library is written and tested by people; a generated path covers anything you ask for. Iro has both — a curated AI library plus Custom Paths for everything else — so ask which half you would actually use.
  • What does the free tier let you actually do? Not whether one exists. A free tier that shows you a tour tells you nothing; one that lets you complete a real lesson tells you everything.

And if the answer to the first question was credential, go back to the exam-prep section above and ignore this whole category.

FAQ

What is the best Kinnu alternative?

It depends which Kinnu you used. For the general-knowledge app, pick by subject: Iro AI if you want to get good at using AI tools, Brilliant for maths and logic, Khan Academy for academic foundations at no cost. For Kinnu's separate exam-prep platform covering the UK SQE1 and CFA Level I, the alternative is another syllabus-aligned question bank, not a general microlearning app.

Is Kinnu free?

We are not going to state Kinnu's pricing, because we could not verify it from Kinnu's own listing at the time of writing and this category is full of pages repeating figures from affiliate sites. Open the App Store listing and read the in-app purchase section, which is authoritative and takes about thirty seconds. The same goes for the exam-prep platform, which is billed separately from the general-knowledge app.

Is Kinnu the same company as the SQE and CFA app?

Yes. Kinnu is a UK company founded in 2021, and it runs both the general-knowledge microlearning app and a separate paid exam-prep platform covering the UK SQE1 and CFA Level I, with a 3,000+ question bank, study notes, flashcards and an AI tutor called LARA. They are distinct products with distinct subscriptions, which is why alternatives pages that treat Kinnu as one app tend to give unhelpful answers.

How is Iro AI different from Kinnu?

Scope and session type. Kinnu's general-knowledge app spans many subjects as a curated library. Iro's curated library is AI-focused — 29 paths, 477 lessons, 3,000+ exercises — and Custom Paths (a Pro feature) generate a path on any topic you name, from a typed subject, a link, a screenshot or a PDF for anything outside it. And where a knowledge pathway explains something and then checks you followed it, Iro's sessions are exercises: writing a prompt and being told which part was weak, spotting a fabricated citation, choosing between two tools and learning why one loses.

Is there a free alternative to Kinnu?

Khan Academy is free with no paid tier and covers academic foundations properly. Iro AI is free to start with every learning path open, paced by a Battery that gives you a lesson a day and recharges overnight, and it also runs as a free web app at app.tryiro.com that needs no signup. Free means very different things across this category, so compare what each tier lets you do rather than whether it exists.

## Why healthcare needs careful AI practice

Healthcare AI use is high-context and high-stakes. The same tools that can summarize a long paper or draft a referral letter can also invent a dosage, mislabel a drug, or quietly drop a contraindication. Iro is designed to build the judgment loop: ask the right question, supply the right context, evaluate the answer, and own the result.

Iro is not medical advice and is not a replacement for clinical training, professional medical education, or your organization's compliance, privacy, and patient-safety policies.
## What you practice in Iro

- Summarizing literature, guidelines, and patient education materials.
- Drafting referral letters, follow-up plans, and patient-facing language.
- Catching hallucinations in dosages, drug names, contraindications, and citations.
- Using AI for administrative work (scheduling, intake, documentation prompts).
- Understanding when AI should not be used in a clinical workflow.

The healthcare path is built for clinicians, residents, medical students, nurses, allied-health professionals, and healthcare operators who handle research, documentation, or patient-facing comms. It pairs well with AI for managers and prompt engineering.

## FAQ

**How can nurses learn to use AI at work?**  
Start with low-risk uses like drafting patient education material, summarizing guidelines and handling admin, and learn the checks before anything clinical. Iro's AI for Healthcare path covers documentation, patient communication, privacy, hallucinations and clinical AI safety in 15 five-minute lessons on iPhone and the web. Follow your employer's AI policy, and never paste identifiable patient information into a public chatbot.

**Is there an app to help nurses learn AI?**

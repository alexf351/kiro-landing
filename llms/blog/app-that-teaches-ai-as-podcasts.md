# Is there an app that teaches AI skills as short podcast episodes?

> Yes. Iro can play any of its AI lessons as a two-to-three-minute podcast episode, then has you practise what you heard. Here's how it works and who it suits.

## Key takeaways

- Iro plays any lesson as a 2 to 3 minute podcast episode, with two hosts, Sam and Ava, talking it through.
- Each episode sits next to a five-minute hands-on lesson, so you listen first and then practise.
- Lesson podcasts are part of Iro Pro; the lessons themselves are free to start, on iPhone and the web.
- Listening alone builds awareness. The skill comes from practising on real tasks straight after.

## The short answer

**Yes. Iro is an app on iPhone and the web that can play any of its AI lessons as a short podcast episode, about two to three minutes long.** Two hosts, Sam and Ava, talk the lesson through, then you do the five-minute hands-on version and get instant feedback on what you tried.

That pairing is the point. You can listen on a walk or a commute, then practise when you have your hands free.

## How the episodes work

- **Length:** two to three minutes per episode, one episode per lesson.
- **Format:** two hosts, Sam and Ava, in a conversation rather than a lecture.
- **Then practice:** each lesson ends in an exercise, like writing a real prompt, spotting the false claim in an AI answer or choosing the right tool, with feedback straight away.
- **Where:** in the Iro app on iPhone and on the web.

## What you can listen to

Iro has 34 learning paths and 522 lessons, and any lesson can be an episode. Paths include tool courses for [ChatGPT](/learn-chatgpt), [Claude](/learn-claude), [Gemini](/learn-gemini), [Microsoft Copilot](/learn-copilot), [Perplexity](/learn-perplexity) and [Cursor](/learn-cursor), a 30-lesson [prompt engineering](/prompt-engineering-app) path, a 33-lesson AI Foundations path for complete beginners, and role paths for [marketing](/ai-for-marketing), [managers](/ai-for-managers), [finance](/ai-for-finance) and more.

## Why listening is not enough

A good AI podcast keeps you up to date, but hearing someone describe a prompt doesn't teach you to write one. The skill shows up when you try it on a real task and see what comes back.

So use the episode as the warm-up and the exercise as the workout. Two to three minutes of listening plus five minutes of practice a day adds up quickly.

## The current numbers: median 7, and only 1 in 7 get a perfect score

**Across 196 people who took our free [AI IQ test](/quiz) between April 1 and October 8, 2026, the median score is 7 out of 10, the average is 6.91, 33% scored 9 or higher (about 1 in 3), and only 14% got a perfect 10 (about 1 in 7).** Scores span the full range from 0 to 10.

The top is genuinely hard to reach: a third of people score 9 or higher, and only about 1 in 7 post a perfect 10. The bottom is still very much occupied: about 4 in 10 people land at 6 or below, and 14% score 3 or below.

## Why the number moves: who shows up

When we published the [first cut of this data](#method), the average across 50 completions was 5.2. A later cut that counted every completion, retakes and our own test runs included, came out at 7.4. This version is stricter, and it lands in between at 6.91. Nobody's AI skills swing that much at population scale, so what is moving?

**The audience.** Where someone arrives from predicts their score better than almost anything else in this data. Visitors who came to the quiz from Reddit averaged 8.4. Visitors who came from Google averaged 6.1. A plausible reason: Reddit visitors tend to arrive from AI-focused communities, while search visitors are closer to cold traffic.

**Retakes and test runs used to count.** Earlier cuts included repeat attempts, and people who retake tend to be chasing a better number. They also included a handful of perfect 10s from our founder testing his own quiz. This time each person counts once, by their first attempt, and internal test runs are out.

This is the quiet lesson of the whole post: with self-selected data, _who shows up_ moves the number far more than how good people are. Any published statistic about "the average person's AI skills", ours included, is really a statistic about who took that particular test.

## What it means: the divide is the finding

Two things are true at once in this data, and both survive every cut we have made of it.

**AI fluency is learnable and plenty of people have it.** A median of 7, with a third of people at 9 or higher, shows the skills are absorbable: prompting, tool choice, knowing when a model is likely to hallucinate. These are not talent, they are reps.

**The spread refuses to close.** About 4 in 10 people land at 6 or below and 14% score 3 or below. The distance between someone who scores 9 and someone who scores 3 is not trivia knowledge, it is the difference between reliably getting trustworthy output from AI and pasting a vague question into a chat box. That divide showed up in the early cold-traffic cohort, and it shows up again in the gap between Reddit and Google visitors.

If AI skills are becoming a workplace dividing line, this is what it looks like in miniature: the people who practise pull ahead, and the gap widens rather than narrows.

## About this data (the honest version)

What this is and is not:

- **Sample:** 196 people who completed the quiz between April 1 and October 8, 2026. Each person counts once, by their first attempt. Retakes are not counted.
- **Exclusions:** internal test runs (our founder and team testing the quiz) are removed.
- **The test:** the Iro [AI IQ test](/quiz), 10 questions spanning prompting, AI tools, and judgment, scored 0 to 10.
- **Current stats:** median 7, average 6.91, middle 50% between 5 and 9, range 0 to 10. 33% scored 9 or higher and 14% scored a perfect 10.
- **Traffic source:** scores depend heavily on where people came from. Reddit visitors averaged 8.4 and Google visitors averaged 6.1.
- **Earlier cohorts:** the first 50 completions (March to June 2026) averaged 5.2 with a median of 5. A later cut that counted retakes and internal runs averaged 7.4. Those snapshots are mentioned above because the difference between them is itself the finding.
- **What it is not:** a representative study. Takers are self-selected, the quiz is web only, and the sample is small. Treat it as an honest, dated snapshot.

We'll keep refreshing these numbers as the sample grows, and we'll keep showing the previous cohorts when we do.

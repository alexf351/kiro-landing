# The best AI literacy apps and resources for teachers in 2026

> Most "AI apps for teachers" lists are about AI that writes your lesson plans. This one is about teaching students to use AI well, and the free curricula that already do it.

**Canonical:** https://tryiro.com/blog/best-ai-literacy-resources-for-teachers
**Published:** 2026-11-06
**Reading time:** ~6 min
**Author:** Alex Furukawa — Founder of Iro AI

## Key takeaways

- Split the question in two: tools that help you plan (Khanmigo, MagicSchool) and curricula that teach students AI literacy. Most lists mix them up.
- For a ready-to-teach unit, the strongest free options are Code.org's How AI Works (grades 6–12), Day of AI (pre-K–12), Experience AI (ages 8–16) and Common Sense Education's lessons.
- If you want something you can print and run without student accounts, Iro's free classroom kit (ours) gives high school teachers five lessons and a two-minute pre and post check.
- Apps for adults, including ours and Synapse, are better for building your own skills first than for running a class.

## The short answer

**The best AI literacy resource for your class depends on grade level and how much setup you can take on.** For middle and high school, Code.org's _How AI Works_ and Day of AI give you full, free units. For ages 8 to 16, Experience AI from the Raspberry Pi Foundation and Google DeepMind is free and well structured. For a printable high school unit that needs no student accounts, Iro's free [AI literacy classroom kit](/ai-literacy-classroom-kit) (ours) runs in five lessons with a two-minute check before and after.

One distinction saves a lot of time: tools that help _you_ plan, like Khanmigo, are not AI literacy curricula. They are useful, but they do not teach students how AI works, where it fails, or how to check it.

## How we chose

We included resources that a teacher can use this term without a district contract, and judged them on four things:

- **Does it teach students AI literacy** (how models work, where they fail, how to check output), or does it help the teacher plan?
- **Grade range**, as the provider states it.
- **Setup**: whether students need accounts, devices or a specific platform.
- **Cost**, from the provider's own site.

Iro's kit is ours. We list it because it fills a specific gap (a short, printable, no-account high school unit), and we hold it to the same four tests.

## Which one to pick

- **You teach middle or high school and want a full unit →** Code.org's How AI Works or Day of AI.
- **You teach ages 8 to 16 and want structured lessons with slides →** Experience AI.
- **You want short, discussion-led lessons you can drop into any subject →** Common Sense Education or Stanford CRAFT.
- **You teach high school, have a week, and can't set up student accounts →** the [Iro classroom kit](/ai-literacy-classroom-kit), with the teaching sequence in [how to teach AI literacy in high school](/blog/teach-ai-literacy-high-school).
- **You want help writing lesson plans →** Khanmigo is free for teachers, but pair it with one of the curricula above.

## Building your own AI skills first

Teaching AI literacy is easier once you use the tools well yourself. Apps for adults fit that job better than the classroom. Iro AI (ours) is free to start, with five-minute lessons on ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini on iPhone and the web, and every path open on the free tier. Synapse is another option for teachers learning on iPhone or iPad, at $2.99 a week after a 14-day trial.

Iro is a self-paced app for individual learners aged 13 and up. It has no class management or student roster, which is why the kit exists as a separate, account-free resource. For more on using AI in your own teaching, see [AI for teachers](/blog/ai-for-teachers).

## FAQ

**What is the best AI literacy app for teachers?**

It depends on the job. For teaching students, the strongest free curricula are Code.org's How AI Works (grades 6–12), Day of AI (pre-K–12) and Experience AI (ages 8–16). For a short printable high school unit with no student accounts, Iro's free classroom kit. For building your own skills first, an app like Iro AI or Synapse.

**Are there free AI literacy lessons for high school?**

Yes. Code.org, Day of AI, Experience AI, aiEDU and Stanford CRAFT all offer free material for high school, and Iro's classroom kit is free with no student accounts.

**Is Khanmigo an AI literacy curriculum?**

No. Khanmigo for teachers is an AI teaching assistant that helps with planning and is free for teachers. It does not replace a curriculum that teaches students how AI works and how to check it.

**Do students need accounts to use these resources?**

Most providers do not say. Iro's classroom kit needs none: it is printable and runs without any student sign-up. aiEDU asks teachers to create a free account to download materials.

# The best prompt engineering courses in 2026 (free and paid)

> Seven prompt engineering courses compared by cost, time and how you learn, from Google and Vanderbilt to Anthropic's free tutorial, plus the part most people skip: actually practicing.

## Key takeaways

- Best structured course for beginners: Google Prompting Essentials on Coursera (under 10 hours, $49 a month after a 7-day trial in the US).
- Best deep course: Vanderbilt's Prompt Engineering for ChatGPT on Coursera (about 18 hours, free to enroll, certificate is paid).
- Best free hands-on tutorial: Anthropic's Prompt Engineering Interactive Tutorial (9 chapters, free).
- Best for developers: DeepLearning.AI's ChatGPT Prompt Engineering for Developers (under 2 hours, free for now).
- Best for daily practice on your phone: Iro (five-minute lessons where you write real prompts and get instant feedback; free to start).

## 1. Google Prompting Essentials

Google's beginner program on Coursera is four short courses built around a simple five-step framework for writing prompts. Coursera rates the program 4.8 from more than 8,000 reviews. It takes under 10 hours, and in the US it costs $49 a month after a 7-day free trial, so finishing fast keeps it cheap.

**Good:** clear framework, recognizable certificate, beginner pace. **Watch out:** mostly video. You will need to practice outside the course.

## 2. Prompt Engineering for ChatGPT (Vanderbilt University)

Taught by Dr. Jules White, this is the deepest beginner course on the list: six modules and about 18 hours of work, rated 4.8 from more than 8,000 reviews. You can enroll for free; the certificate needs Coursera's paid certificate option or Coursera Plus.

**Good:** covers prompt patterns in depth, strong instructor. **Watch out:** long. Many people stall around week two.

## 3. Anthropic's Prompt Engineering Interactive Tutorial

Anthropic's free tutorial has nine chapters in three levels, from basic structure to complex prompts. Each lesson ends in a playground where you edit prompts and see what changes, and there is an answer key.

**Good:** the most hands-on free option, and the skills carry over to any chatbot. **Watch out:** it is built as notebooks, which can feel technical for non-developers.

## 4. Anthropic AI Fluency courses

A free, self-paced course on working with AI well: delegating, describing tasks and checking output. The main course is 12 modules and takes 3 to 4 hours, and the exercises use Claude or any chatbot you prefer. Anthropic also offers free versions for students, educators and nonprofits on Coursera.

**Good:** broader than prompting, useful for work. **Watch out:** less drilling on prompt writing itself.

## 5. ChatGPT Prompt Engineering for Developers (DeepLearning.AI)

A short course from DeepLearning.AI, built with OpenAI and taught by Isa Fulford and Andrew Ng. It runs about 1 hour 40 minutes and covers writing clear instructions, iterating on prompts, and using prompts to summarize, infer, transform and expand text, with code notebooks. DeepLearning.AI says access is free for a limited time while its learning platform is in beta.

**Good:** short and free, great if you build with the API. **Watch out:** written for developers.

## 6. Learn Prompting

A large free written guide to prompting techniques, with paid courses on top. Best used as a reference you return to, not a course you finish.

## 7. Iro (for daily practice)

Iro is not a course. It is where you practice what a course teaches. Each lesson takes about five minutes: you write a real prompt, get instant feedback on it, and move on. Streaks, XP and 16 ranks keep you coming back. It is free to start and runs on [iPhone](/learn-ai-on-iphone) and [the web](https://app.tryiro.com).

**Good:** turns a course you watched into a skill you use, in five minutes a day. **Watch out:** it will not give you a university certificate. Pair it with one of the courses above if you need one.

## Which should you pick?

- **New to AI and want a certificate:** Google Prompting Essentials, plus five minutes a day in Iro.
- **Want to go deep for free:** Anthropic's interactive tutorial, then Vanderbilt's course.
- **A developer:** DeepLearning.AI's short course, then Anthropic's tutorial.

## FAQ

**What is the best free prompt engineering course?**

Anthropic's Prompt Engineering Interactive Tutorial is the best free option if you want to practice, because every lesson has you edit prompts yourself. DeepLearning.AI's short course (under 2 hours) is the best free option for developers.

**Is a prompt engineering certificate worth it?**

A certificate helps on a resume, but employers care more about whether you can get good output from AI. Pair a certificate course with regular practice. See our guide to whether AI certifications are worth it.

**How long does it take to learn prompt engineering?**

Most people learn the basics in 5 to 10 hours of study, then get good with a few weeks of regular practice. Short daily practice beats one long course.

**Can I learn prompt engineering on my phone?**

Yes. Iro teaches prompting in five-minute lessons on iPhone and the web, where you write real prompts and get instant feedback.

**Is prompt engineering still worth learning in 2026?**

Yes, though the skill has shifted from tricks to clear thinking: giving context, describing the task and checking the output. See our post on whether prompt engineering is dead.

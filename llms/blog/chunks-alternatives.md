## How to read a roundup written by one of the apps in it

Search for the best microlearning apps and look at who owns the pages that rank. In our own check of that result page, the top results were app blogs: _nibble-app.com_, _nerdsip.com_, _chunks.app_, _morso.app_, plus a couple of affiliate sites and a Medium post. No newspaper. No independent consumer publication. Every ranking page was published by a company selling one of the products on it.

That is not a scandal, it is what happens when a category has no editorial gatekeeper. But it means the ordinary reading of a listicle, that someone impartial compared these and picked a winner, is false in almost every case. Three tells tell you whether a conflicted roundup is still worth reading.

Undisclosed self-ranking is the whole problem. A page that tells you in the first paragraph that it is published by one of the apps has at least given you what you need to discount it.

A genuinely useful comparison names the cases where its own product is the wrong choice, and means it. If every reader path terminates at the publisher's app, you are reading an advert with a table in it.

Look at the prices and ratings. Are they sourced, dated, and consistent with the store listing you can open right now? A large share of the numbers circulating in this category originate on affiliate pages and get copied between roundups until five sources agree, which is one unverified claim repeated five times.

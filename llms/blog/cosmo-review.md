# Cosmo review: CodeSignal's AI tutor app, what it costs, and who it suits

> Cosmo is CodeSignal's bite-sized learning app, pitched as Duolingo for job skills. It comes from an established company, the courses are free, and the terms are some of the cleaner ones in this category. Here is who makes it, how it teaches, what Cosmo+ costs, and who it suits.

## Key takeaways

- Cosmo is made by CodeSignal, Inc., the San Francisco company behind the CodeSignal technical hiring assessments. It launched in August 2025 on iPhone, with Android following a week later.
- Every course is free. What you pay for is Cosmo+, which removes the energy limit on how much you can practise in a day.
- Launch coverage priced Cosmo+ at $24.99 a month or $149.99 a year, and both prices still appear on the App Store. The listing also shows $12.99 and $78.00 options without saying what they are.
- It is broad: 200+ skills across GenAI, business, coding and data. At launch, nearly a third of the content was about generative AI.
- Purchases go through the App Store or Google Play, so you cancel and request refunds there. CodeSignal's own terms say it does not give refunds unless the law requires it.

## The short verdict

Cosmo is a legitimate, well-funded product from a company that has been around for a decade. The courses are free, billing goes through Apple or Google, and its terms are clearer than most of the AI course apps we have reviewed. The trade-offs are breadth and price: AI is one subject among many, and Cosmo+ costs more than most apps in this space.

If you want a light daily habit across several career skills, including coding, it is a reasonable pick. If your goal is specifically to get good at using AI tools at work, a focused app will take you further for less.

Every claim here comes from Cosmo's App Store and Google Play listings, CodeSignal's own pages and Terms of Use (effective August 2025), and VentureBeat's launch coverage. We make a competing app, so weigh our view accordingly. Last reviewed November 2026.

## Who makes Cosmo

Cosmo is made by **CodeSignal, Inc.**, a Delaware corporation based in San Francisco. It is the same name you see as the seller on the App Store and in the Terms of Use the listing links to, so there is no question about who you are dealing with.

CodeSignal started in 2015 as CodeFights and took its current name in 2018. It is best known for the coding assessments companies use when hiring engineers, and it raised a $50 million Series C led by Index Ventures in 2021. In the app listing, CodeSignal says its platform is used by more than 3,000 employers and more than a million learners.

VentureBeat reported the launch on August 20, 2025, quoting co-founder and CEO Tigran Sloyan, with Android following on August 28. Companies that already pay for CodeSignal's learning platform get Cosmo as part of their subscription. Support is by email at the address on the App Store listing.

## How Cosmo teaches

Cosmo calls its approach practice first: no videos, a few minutes a day, and an AI tutor that adapts to your level. VentureBeat described lessons that drop you into realistic workplace scenarios, and a tutor you can interrupt with a question at any point, get an explanation, then return to the lesson. The app adds the usual habit tools: streaks, skill badges and shareable certificates.

The range is wide. The listing claims 200+ skills, and launch coverage said 300+ courses, with CodeSignal's own app page using both numbers. Subjects include:

- GenAI, including practising prompts and applying ChatGPT at work
- business: marketing, sales, finance, accounting, product and project management, leadership
- soft skills such as communication, public speaking, decision making and focus
- programming in Python, JavaScript, Go, Swift, Kotlin and Rust, plus SQL, cloud and web development
- data science, analytics, machine learning and data engineering

At launch, nearly a third of the content was about generative AI. That is a meaningful share, but it means most of the catalogue is about something else. We did not pay for Cosmo+, so we are not rating the lessons themselves. A fair test in your first week: are you writing and testing prompts yourself, or mostly answering questions about them?

## What it costs

Cosmo is free to download, and its App Store listing says every course is free. The limit is **energy**: practice uses it up, and when it runs out you wait. **Cosmo+** is the paid upgrade, and the listing describes it as unlimited energy so you can learn faster.

VentureBeat's launch report put Cosmo+ at **$24.99 a month or $149.99 a year**, and both prices still appear in the US App Store's in-app purchase list. The same list shows two more options, at **$12.99** and **$78.00**, all four named Unlimited Access, with no label saying what period each covers. They may be offers or different plan lengths; check what the paywall shows you before you tap.

Cosmo+ is sold in the app through Apple or Google. That matters: the store shows the renewal terms at checkout, and you manage or cancel the subscription in your phone's settings.

## What the terms say

The App Store listing links to CodeSignal's general Terms of Use, effective August 20, 2025. They do not mention Cosmo by name, but they cover paid services and in-app purchases. The points worth knowing:

- **Apple or Google handles the purchase.** The terms say in-app purchases go through Apple or Google, and CodeSignal is not a party to them. So refunds for an App Store purchase are Apple's call, through reportaproblem.apple.com.
- **No refunds from CodeSignal** for paid services unless the law where you live requires one. Its help center lists those countries; in many European countries and the UK the window is 14 days from the start of a subscription.
- **Automatic renewal.** Plans renew for the same period at the then-current non-promotional rate until you cancel, and you keep access until the end of the period you paid for.
- **Arbitration.** Disputes go to individual arbitration with JAMS in San Francisco, with a class-action and jury waiver, small claims court excepted. CodeSignal pays the arbitration fees for claims under $10,000.
- **You can opt out of arbitration** by posting a written notice to CodeSignal's San Francisco address within 30 days of first accepting the terms, with your name, address and the email or phone number on your account.
- **Age.** Although the App Store rates Cosmo 4+, the terms say children under 13 should not register.

The App Store privacy label also says some identifiers may be used to track you across other companies' apps and websites. You can decline when iOS asks to allow tracking.

Compared with the web-sold AI course apps we have covered, such as [Skilario](/blog/skilario-review), there is no money-back guarantee with conditions to meet and no website checkout to untangle. The trade-off is that CodeSignal offers no guarantee of its own: if you want your money back, it is between you and the store.

## How to cancel Cosmo+

Deleting the app does not cancel Cosmo+. Cancel through the store you bought it from:

- **iPhone:** open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions, then Cosmo, and cancel.
- **Android:** open the Google Play Store, tap your profile, then Payments & subscriptions, then Subscriptions, then Cosmo.

Apple renews a subscription unless you cancel at least 24 hours before the period ends, so on either platform do it a day or two early. You keep Cosmo+ until the end of the period, and the free courses stay available after that.

## Who Cosmo suits

- **Good fit:** you want a light daily learning habit across several work skills, you like a tutor you can ask questions, and you are curious about coding or data as well as AI. If your employer already pays for CodeSignal's learning platform, you may have Cosmo included.
- **Less of a fit:** you want to get properly good at using AI tools at work. Cosmo covers that, but as roughly a third of a broad catalogue, and its paid tier costs more than most AI-focused apps.

Before you pay, use the free courses for a week. If the energy limit is the only thing in your way, Cosmo+ is a simple purchase to undo. Our guide to [spotting an AI subscription trap](/blog/ai-subscription-traps) covers the checks worth making with any learning app.

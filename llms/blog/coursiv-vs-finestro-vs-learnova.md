# Coursiv vs Finestro vs Learnova: which is worth paying for?

> Three AI learning apps that look almost identical, sold the same way. Here is what each one costs, what its refund policy actually says, and how to judge any of them before you enter a card.

## Key takeaways

- These are not three different products so much as three versions of one format: a quiz funnel, a cheap first week, then a much larger recurring charge.
- Coursiv's money-back guarantee is conditional on using the app. On the 4-week and 12-week plans you must finish 4 guides or 80% of modules to qualify, and supply screenshots of your progress.
- Finestro's published policy states that forgetting to cancel, or not knowing about auto-renewal, do not qualify for a refund. It runs about $10 for a week, then roughly $49.99 a month.
- Finestro and Learnova ship under near-identical names, both built on 'AI Tools Mastery', from two unrelated developers.
- Judge any of them, ours included, on three things you can check in under a minute: the renewal price, the renewal date, and where the cancel button is.

## Short answer

**On content, there is less between these three than the marketing suggests.** All of them walk a beginner through ChatGPT and a handful of other AI tools with short, readable lessons. If you only want a tour of what the tools do, any of them will do that.

The real difference is what happens on day eight. That is where the three separate, and it is why almost everyone arrives at a page like this one. So this comparison spends most of its time on pricing and refund terms rather than on curriculum, because that is the part that actually varies and the part nobody publishes side by side.

## The shared playbook

Read the three sign-up flows back to back and the same structure appears every time:

- **A quiz.** Ten or so questions about your job and your goals, which produce a personalised plan. The quiz has no effect on the price.
- **A cheap first week.** Usually somewhere around $1 to $10, presented as the headline number.
- **A much larger recurring charge** once that week ends, typically five to fifty times the intro price.
- **A refund policy with conditions** that are easy to miss at sign-up and hard to satisfy afterwards.

How closely they converge is easiest to see in the names. Finestro ships as **Finestro: AI Tools Mastery**. Learnova ships as **AI Tools Mastery - Learnova**. Same phrase, same category, two unrelated developers: Finestro's Android build is published under `com.applyft.finestro.AI`, Learnova's under `com.codeway.courseai`, with the App Store listing crediting DEEP FLOW SOFTWARE SERVICES.

That matters for how you read reviews. You are not choosing between three companies with three philosophies. You are choosing between three implementations of one funnel, which is why comparing their lesson counts is close to useless and comparing their billing terms tells you everything.

## What the terms actually say

Everything below comes from each company's own published policy or its store listing. Where something is a user account rather than a documented fact, it says so.

Coursiv advertises a money-back guarantee, and it is real, but it is **conditional on how much of the app you used**. From its published refund policy, to qualify you must have:

- **1-week plan:** used the app at least 3 days, and completed 2 guides or 70% of modules
- **4-week plan:** used the app at least 7 days, and completed 4 guides or 80% of modules
- **12-week plan:** used the app at least 10 days, and completed 4 guides or 80% of modules

Requests go to support within 30 days of purchase, with screenshots showing your progress. The policy states that dissatisfaction on its own, or a billing complaint without meeting the usage criteria, is not covered.

Read that again in practical terms. If you subscribe, dislike it on day two and stop opening it, you have disqualified yourself from the guarantee by not using it enough. The people most likely to want a refund are the people least likely to qualify for one. That is not hidden and it is not illegal. It is just the opposite of what most people hear in the phrase 'money-back guarantee'. If you are already subscribed, see [how to cancel Coursiv and get your money back](/blog/cancel-coursiv).

Finestro runs about **$10 for a one-week trial, then roughly $49.99 a month**. Its published refund policy lists forgetting to cancel before the trial converts, and being unaware of auto-renewal, as reasons that do _not_ qualify for a refund. The trial deadline is entirely your responsibility.

Separately, and at a lower level of certainty: App Store reviewers and people filing complaints with the Better Business Bureau describe charges continuing after cancellation requests and refunds being refused. Those are user accounts, not findings. Set against them, the app holds roughly **4.76 out of 5 across about 320 App Store ratings**, so plenty of customers have no complaint at all.

Learnova is the hardest of the three to pin down, which is itself the finding. Its pricing and refund terms are not published anywhere we could verify, and its App Store listing carries too few ratings to show a rating overview at all.

We are not going to invent numbers to fill the row. If you are considering Learnova, open the App Store listing, tap through to the subscription details before you subscribe, and screenshot the renewal price and date. If a price is hard to find before you pay, treat that as information.

## Are any of them scams?

No, and it is worth being precise about this, because the word gets thrown around in reviews and it points people at the wrong question.

All three are real companies shipping real apps with real content, on both major app stores, with support addresses that answer. A scam takes your money and delivers nothing. These deliver lessons, and a good number of people finish them and are glad they paid.

What they run is something else: a subscription funnel where the number you agree to is smaller than the number you pay, and the conditions for getting out are set at sign-up rather than at the point you want to leave. That is a legitimate business model used by thousands of apps, including some you already pay for. It is also the reason a well-liked app with a 4.76 rating can generate a steady flow of billing complaints. Both things are true at once.

The useful question is not whether an app is a scam. It is whether you can find the renewal price before you pay, and whether cancelling takes two taps or an email thread.

## The 60-second check for any of them

Before you enter a card into any AI learning app, including ours, find these four things. All of them are visible before you pay, and any app that makes one hard to find has told you something.

- **The renewal price.** Not the intro price. The number you pay in week two.
- **The renewal date.** Put it in your calendar with a reminder a day early. This single step prevents most of the complaints in this article.
- **Where cancelling happens.** If it is your iPhone subscription settings, that is two taps and cannot be refused. If it is an email to support, that is a conversation someone else controls.
- **What the refund policy requires of you.** Not whether one exists. What you have to have done to qualify.

If you subscribed to something through the App Store and want out, that is a different job and worth doing properly: see [how to cancel and request a refund](/blog/cancel-finestro), which works the same way for any App Store subscription.

## FAQ

**Which is better, Coursiv, Finestro or Learnova?**

On content they are close enough that the choice rarely comes down to lessons. Finestro publishes the clearest pricing of the three, at roughly $10 for a week then about $49.99 a month, and holds around 4.76 out of 5 from about 320 App Store ratings. Coursiv publishes the most detailed refund policy, though it requires you to have completed up to 80% of modules to qualify. Learnova publishes the least, with no verifiable pricing and too few ratings to show an overview.

**Is Coursiv's money-back guarantee real?**

Yes, but it is conditional on usage. Coursiv's published policy requires that you used the app at least 3 days and completed 2 guides or 70% of modules on the 1-week plan, or at least 7 to 10 days and 4 guides or 80% of modules on the longer plans, and that you request the refund within 30 days with screenshots of your progress. Dissatisfaction alone, without meeting the usage criteria, is not covered.

**Is Finestro or Coursiv a scam?**

No. Both are real companies with real apps, real content and working support. The recurring complaint is about subscription mechanics rather than the product: a low intro price that converts to a much higher recurring charge, and refund conditions set at sign-up. Read the renewal price and date before you subscribe, and note where the cancel button lives.

**Are Finestro and Learnova the same company?**

No. They ship under near-identical names, Finestro: AI Tools Mastery and AI Tools Mastery - Learnova, but they are separate developers. Finestro's Android build is published under com.applyft.finestro.AI, while Learnova's is com.codeway.courseai with the App Store listing crediting DEEP FLOW SOFTWARE SERVICES. The similarity is in the format they both copied, not in the ownership.

**What is the cheapest way to learn AI properly?**

Free, in most cases. Khan Academy is fully free, Iro AI is free to start on iOS with a battery that gives you a full lesson every day and recharges overnight, and the AI rank quiz needs no signup or card at all. Paying is worth it when it buys you structure and a daily habit, not when it buys you a list of tools you could have read about anywhere.

**How do I cancel one of these subscriptions?**

If you subscribed inside the app on an iPhone, cancel in Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions, then the app, then Cancel Subscription. On Android, use the Play Store under Payments and subscriptions. If you subscribed on the company's website instead, cancelling happens in your account settings on that site, and a refund means emailing their support.

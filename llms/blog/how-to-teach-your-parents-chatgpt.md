# What app can my parents use to learn ChatGPT step by step?

> There's no single app that teaches ChatGPT and runs it too. Here's the simple setup, the first five things to try, what never to share, and a two-week plan.

## The short answer

**Your parents will use ChatGPT in the free ChatGPT app itself, and a short-lesson app like Iro can teach them the basics step by step, about five minutes a day.** There's no single app that does both, so the simplest plan is: set up the official ChatGPT app with them, show them the voice button, then give them one small thing to try each day.

The rest of this guide is that plan: a ten-minute setup, the first five things to try, what never to share, and a two-week routine.

## Set it up together in ten minutes

- **Install the real app.** Search for ChatGPT in the App Store or Google Play and check that the developer is OpenAI. Copycat apps with similar names and icons exist, and some charge for what the real app gives away.
- **Make an account with them.** Use an email address they actually check, and let them pick the password themselves.
- **Put it on the home screen** where they'll see it, next to the apps they already use every day.
- **Show them the voice button.** Many older adults find talking to ChatGPT far easier than typing. They can ask a question out loud and hear the answer read back.
- **Ask one real question together** before you leave, ideally about something they care about, so the first experience is useful rather than a demo.

## The first five things to try

Give them one of these a day. Each is a real task with a clear result, which beats "just play with it."

- **Cook from what's in the fridge:** "I have chicken, rice, carrots and an onion. Give me an easy dinner for two with step-by-step instructions."
- **Write a message:** "Help me write a short, warm birthday message for my granddaughter, who just started college."
- **Explain something in plain words:** "Explain what a 'deductible' means on my car insurance, like you're talking to a friend."
- **Plan a day out:** "Plan a relaxed day trip from our town for two people who don't like long walks."
- **Learn about a hobby:** "I want to start growing tomatoes on my balcony. Walk me through it one step at a time and wait for me to say next."

The last one shows the most useful habit: asking ChatGPT to go one step at a time and wait. It turns a wall of text into a conversation.

## What never to share, and when to double-check

Go through this out loud with them. It matters more than any prompt.

- **Never type or say:** passwords, bank or card numbers, Social Security or Medicare numbers, or anything they'd only tell their bank.
- **ChatGPT can be confidently wrong.** For anything about health, medicine, money or the law, treat the answer as a starting point and check it with a doctor, pharmacist, bank or advisor.
- **Watch for scams that use the name.** No AI company will phone or text them asking for a password or a payment. If someone does, it's a scam.
- **If an answer sounds odd, ask it:** "How sure are you? What should I double-check?" It's a good habit for anyone.

## A simple two-week plan

- **Days 1 to 5:** one of the five tasks above each day, by voice if they prefer.
- **Days 6 and 7:** they pick their own question. Ask them what they tried.
- **Week 2:** one five-minute lesson a day, plus one real question to ChatGPT about something coming up that week, like a trip, a recipe or a letter to answer.
- **End of week 2:** sit down together and have them show you something they now use it for. That's the sign it stuck.

## FAQ

**What app can my parents use to learn ChatGPT step by step?**

They'll use ChatGPT in the free official ChatGPT app from OpenAI, and a short-lesson app like Iro can teach the basics step by step in about five minutes a day. Iro's ChatGPT path has 26 lessons, including one on voice mode and one on avoiding wrong answers, on iPhone and the web, and it's free to start.

**Is ChatGPT safe for older adults to use?**

Yes, with two habits: never share passwords, bank or card numbers, or ID numbers like Social Security or Medicare, and double-check anything about health, money or the law with a real professional, because ChatGPT can be confidently wrong.

**Do my parents need to pay for ChatGPT?**

No. The free version of the ChatGPT app is enough to learn the basics and handle everyday questions. Paid plans add more usage and features, but they aren't needed to get started.

**Can my parents talk to ChatGPT instead of typing?**

Yes. The ChatGPT app has a voice mode: tap the voice button, ask a question out loud, and hear the answer read back. Many older adults find it the easiest way to start.

**How do I stop my parents downloading a fake ChatGPT app?**

Install it with them, and check that the developer listed in the App Store or Google Play is OpenAI. Copycat apps use similar names and icons, and some charge for features the real app gives away free.

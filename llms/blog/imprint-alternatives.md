## Why visual learning feels better than it works

This is not a criticism of any particular app. It is a property of the format, and it is well documented.

A well-made illustration removes cognitive load. The idea arrives assembled, you feel the click, and the feeling is genuinely pleasant. The problem is that **the click is a measure of presentation quality, not of your learning**. Researchers call this the illusion of fluency: material presented clearly gets rated as better learned than material you had to struggle with, and then performs worse on a delayed test. Difficulty during study, within reason, is what makes recall durable later.

Which produces the specific experience people describe when they look for an alternative: forty illustrated lessons on decision-making, complete confidence in the concepts, and no change at all in how they actually make decisions.

The fix is not a sharper diagram. It is **retrieval**, producing the answer yourself before you are shown it, and **feedback**, finding out precisely which part you got wrong. A format where you generate rather than recognise is doing something structurally different, and it is the difference that matters after a month.

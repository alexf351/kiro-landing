# Iro vs Jobescape: which AI learning app is worth it?

> Both promise to make you useful with AI at work in a few minutes a day. They teach differently, they bill differently, and each is better at something. Here is the honest side-by-side.

**Canonical:** https://tryiro.com/blog/iro-ai-vs-jobescape
**Published:** 2026-11-21
**Reading time:** ~6 min
**Author:** Alex Furukawa — Founder of Iro AI

## Key takeaways

- Iro is practice-first: you write and fix real prompts and get them graded. Jobescape is course-first: role-based lessons you read or listen to, with AI tools built in.
- Price: Iro Pro is $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year, free to start. Jobescape's App Store Premium is $29.99 a month or $117.99 a year, and its website prices plans at checkout after a quiz.
- Cancelling: on iPhone, Iro is billed by Apple, so you cancel in Settings anytime. Jobescape web plans must be cancelled in Plan Management more than 24 hours before the period ends.
- Jobescape is better on breadth: 15+ role-based paths, an Android app, 17 languages, audio lessons and a much larger review base.
- Neither app promises income. Jobescape's terms disclaim any guarantee of earnings, and Iro makes no income claims.

## Who Iro is for

- **For:** People who want to get good at ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and prompting by practising for about five minutes a day, with predictable pricing.
- **What it fixes:** Knowing about AI but getting generic answers from it. You write real prompts, fix weak outputs and catch made-up facts, with feedback each time.
- **Not a fit if:** you want role-specific courses you can listen to, a native Android app, lessons in your own language beyond English, or a project-based certificate.

## What Jobescape does well

Jobescape started as an AI-for-freelancing course and now pitches itself as the fastest way to learn Claude and AI for the work you already do. Its strengths are real:

- **Role-based paths.** More than 15 tracks for jobs like legal, HR, finance, sales, accounting, teaching and project management. If you want AI taught in the context of your exact job, Jobescape goes deeper than we do.
- **Read or listen.** Lessons work as audio, with progress synced across devices.
- **AI tools inside the app.** ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity are built in, with pre-made assistants.
- **Certificates.** Shareable, verifiable certificates you earn through projects and can post on LinkedIn.
- **Reach.** iPhone, Android and the web, in 17 languages, with thousands of mostly positive reviews.

Our [Jobescape review](/blog/jobescape-review) covers who runs it and what its terms say in more depth.

## How Iro is different

Iro starts from a different belief: you get good at AI by using it, not by reading about it. So every lesson ends in something you do. You write a prompt in the Prompt Lab and it is graded against a live model, you fix a weak output, or you spot a made-up fact. Streaks, ranks and timed duels against a skill-matched opponent keep the habit going, and Ask Iro, the built-in coach, explains your mistakes by chat or voice.

Iro has **34 learning paths, 522 lessons and 3,000+ exercises across 23 exercise types**, covering ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, prompting, agents and AI for specific jobs. Custom Paths turn any topic you type into a short path of its own, and you can claim certificates as you finish paths.

A fair test for either app, in your first session: are you writing and testing prompts yourself, or mostly reading about them? That is the difference between knowing about AI and being good at it.

## Price and cancelling, side by side

This is where the two differ most, and where most complaints about Jobescape sit.

**Jobescape.** On the US App Store, Premium is $29.99 a month, $59.99 for three months or $117.99 a year, with add-ons such as an AI Toolkit ($29.99) and a Prompts Library ($49.99). On the website, prices appear at checkout after an onboarding quiz. Its Subscription Terms say intro offers convert to the full plan price automatically, and that you must cancel more than 24 hours before the period ends, because the next payment is taken 24 hours before. Recent negative reviews describe extra bundles added at checkout. Its money-back guarantee needs 28 days in a row of documented use in your first month.

**Iro.** Iro is **free to start**, with every path open and a battery that gives you a full lesson every day and recharges overnight. Pro is **$9.99 a month or $49.99 a year**, one price with no intro rate that steps up later, and the annual plan includes a 7-day free trial. On iPhone it is billed by Apple, so you cancel anytime in Settings in two taps. There are no add-on bundles. That makes Iro's monthly plan a third of Jobescape's App Store monthly price, and its yearly plan less than half.

**Neither promises income.** Jobescape's homepage talks about skills you can offer as services, but it publishes no earnings figures, its terms disclaim any guarantee of earnings, and its help center says it does not provide jobs. Iro makes no income or job claims at all.

Already on Jobescape and want out? See [how to cancel Jobescape and ask for a refund](/blog/cancel-jobescape).

Prices are US figures as listed on November 21, 2026 and change without notice, ours included.

## FAQ

**Is Iro better than Jobescape?**

For hands-on practice and simple pricing, yes: Iro grades the prompts you write and costs $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year. Jobescape is better if you want role-specific courses you can listen to, an Android app, 17 languages or a project-based certificate.

**Is Iro cheaper than Jobescape?**

Yes. Iro Pro is $9.99 a month or $49.99 a year and is free to start. Jobescape's App Store Premium is $29.99 a month, $59.99 for three months or $117.99 a year, with add-ons sold separately.

**Which is easier to cancel, Iro or Jobescape?**

On iPhone, both App Store subscriptions cancel in Settings. Jobescape's web plans must be cancelled in Plan Management more than 24 hours before the period ends. Iro on iPhone is billed by Apple, so you cancel in Settings anytime.

**Does Jobescape promise you can earn money?**

It publishes no earnings figures. Its homepage talks about skills you can offer as services, but its terms disclaim any guarantee of earnings and its help center says it does not provide jobs.

**Is Iro on Android like Jobescape?**

Not as a native app yet. Iro runs on iPhone and on the web, so Android users can use it in a browser. Jobescape has native iPhone and Android apps plus a web app.

## Key takeaways

- Ignore feature lists. Four checks decide whether a microlearning app works for you: what a session asks of you, how the content is made, what the free tier actually unlocks, and how it behaves on the day you miss.
- Generated content is the right answer for breadth and the wrong one for depth. If you want to get good at a specific thing, curated beats generated.
- The strongest signal is whether the session is reading-shaped or doing-shaped. Everything else follows from that.
- Iro AI is the pick if the specific thing is using AI: 29 curated paths, 477 lessons and 2,700+ hands-on exercises, five minutes a day.

## The short answer

**Pick by subject, not by app.** Microlearning is a format, not a category, and the format is roughly the same everywhere: short daily sessions, a streak, a progress bar. What actually differs is what you are learning and whether the session makes you do something.

So: **Iro AI** if the subject is using AI tools well. **Brilliant** for maths, logic and computer science. **Khan Academy** if you want academic foundations and no bill. And if what you liked about Morso was generating a course on any topic you can name, the closest thing on that specific axis is [NerdSip](/blog/nerdsip-alternatives), which we have written about separately.

## Why people look for a Morso alternative

The reasons people give for leaving any app in this category are remarkably consistent, and only one of them is really about the app.

- **The streak became the point.** You are opening it daily and retaining nothing. That is a design outcome, not a discipline failure: if the fastest way to keep a streak is to tap through a lesson, most people will.
- **The content felt thin on the subject they cared about.** Broad libraries are broad. The moment you get serious about one topic, a general app runs out of depth before you run out of interest.
- **Billing.** The usual. Find the renewal price and the cancel path before you subscribe to anything, here or anywhere.

## The four checks, in about ten minutes

Run these on any candidate, including ours. They are ordered by how much they predict.

Open one lesson. Count how many seconds you spend consuming versus producing. If the whole session is text and the interaction is a multiple-choice check on what you just read, the app is a reading product with a quiz attached. That is fine for interest and poor for skill. A doing-shaped session asks you to produce something, then tells you what was wrong with it.

Generated content wins on breadth: type any topic and get something back. Curated content wins on depth, because someone who has taught the subject knows which mistakes learners actually make and can build an exercise that catches them. Neither is better in the abstract. Match it to whether you are exploring or committing.

Not whether one exists. Can you complete a real lesson, including the feedback, without a card? A free tier that shows you a tour of the interface tells you nothing about the thing you would be paying for.

Everyone misses. Some apps reset your streak to zero and quietly make it your fault; some hand you a freeze; some do not use streaks at all. There is no correct answer, but there is a correct answer _for you_, and it is worth knowing before you have thirty days invested.

## FAQ

**What is the best Morso alternative?**

It depends on the subject you want. For using AI tools well, Iro AI: 29 curated paths, 477 lessons and 2,700+ hands-on exercises in five-minute sessions. For maths, logic and computer science, Brilliant. For academic foundations at no cost, Khan Academy. If what you specifically wanted was generating a course on any topic you type, that is a different product shape and NerdSip is the closer comparison.

**Is Morso free?**

We could not verify Morso's current pricing or free tier from the company's own listing, so we are not going to print a figure. Prices in this category change often and vary by region, and a large share of the pages that quote them are copying affiliate sites. Open the App Store listing, read the in-app purchase section, and check the renewal price rather than the intro price.

**How do I know if a microlearning app will actually work for me?**

Open one lesson and count how much of it you spend consuming versus producing. Reading-shaped sessions build familiarity; doing-shaped sessions build skill, because you find out what you got wrong while it still matters. Then check whether the free tier lets you finish a real lesson including the feedback, and what happens on a day you miss. Those three answers predict more than any feature list.

**Are AI-generated courses worse than curated ones?**

Not worse, different. Generated content is unbeatable for breadth: you can get a structured introduction to almost anything in seconds, which is genuinely useful when you are exploring. What it cannot do is know which mistakes learners actually make on a subject, or design the exercise that catches them, because nobody taught it that subject specifically. For something you intend to get good at rather than just meet, curated goes further.

**Is Iro AI a microlearning app?**

Yes, in format: five-minute daily sessions, streaks, XP and six ranks. Its curated library is narrower than most, covering using AI tools well across 29 paths and 2,700+ exercises, while Custom Paths (a Pro feature) generate a path on any topic you name, from a typed subject, a link, a screenshot or a PDF. That narrowness is the point. It also runs as a free web app at app.tryiro.com with no signup, so you can judge the format in about two minutes.

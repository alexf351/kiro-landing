## Why ChatGPT takes practice

Most people learn ChatGPT by experimenting randomly. That works for a while, but it usually creates a ceiling: prompts stay vague, outputs are accepted too quickly, and users do not build a repeatable process.

Iro gives ChatGPT practice a structure. You learn what to ask, how much context to provide, how to define success, and how to inspect the answer before using it.

The goal is not memorizing magic prompts. The goal is judgment.

## A 5-minute daily routine to learn ChatGPT

Five minutes works when every minute has a job. Do this once a day, ideally at the same time:

1. **Minute 1, recap:** say out loud, or type, one thing you learned yesterday and one prompt that worked.
2. **Minutes 2 to 3, practice:** do two short prompt exercises. Write a prompt, read the answer, then rewrite the prompt once to make the answer better.
3. **Minute 4, check:** pick one claim in ChatGPT's answer and check it. Ask "How sure are you?" or look it up. This is how you learn to spot hallucinations.
4. **Minute 5, next step:** choose tomorrow's focus (context, examples, format or checking) and stop. Stopping on time is what keeps the streak alive.

Iro's daily lesson covers the practice minutes for you: a short lesson with hands-on prompt exercises, instant feedback on your answers, and a streak that brings you back tomorrow.

### Your first week

| 3 | Ask for a format | "Answer as a table with columns [A], [B], [C]." |
| 4 | Break it into steps | "Before answering, list the steps you'll take." |
| 5 | Make it check itself | "Review your answer. What might be wrong or missing?" |
| 6 | Iterate | Take day 1's prompt and improve it twice. |
| 7 | Real task | Use ChatGPT on one real task from your week, start to finish. |
